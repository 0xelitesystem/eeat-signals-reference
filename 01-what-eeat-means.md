# What E-E-A-T means

E-E-A-T stands for experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness. Experience is firsthand involvement with the topic; expertise is knowledge and skill; authoritativeness is recognition by others as a go-to source; trustworthiness is honesty, accuracy, and reliability. Together they describe content worth believing.

## The four qualities

Experience asks whether the content comes from someone who has actually done or used the thing. Expertise asks whether the author knows the subject deeply. Authoritativeness asks whether others recognize the author or site as a respected source. Trustworthiness asks whether the content and the site are honest, accurate, and safe to rely on. Each addresses a different reason a reader might or might not trust what they read.

## Why it exists

Search and AI systems need a way to judge which content deserves to be surfaced and cited, especially when the stakes for the reader are high. E-E-A-T is the framework for that judgment: it describes the qualities that separate content worth trusting from content that merely exists. Systems look for signals of these qualities because they correlate with content that genuinely helps and does not mislead.

## The added E

Experience was added to the original three because firsthand involvement matters distinctly from book knowledge. Someone who has actually used a product, lived in a place, or done a procedure can offer something a knowledgeable but inexperienced writer cannot. The distinction recognizes that for many topics, having done it carries weight beyond knowing about it. Experience and expertise reinforce each other but are not the same.

## Demonstrated, not declared

A crucial point is that E-E-A-T is demonstrated through signals, not claimed by assertion. Saying you are an expert does not establish expertise; showing depth, accuracy, real experience, and recognition does. The rest of this reference is about the concrete signals that demonstrate each quality, because the framework rewards evidence of these qualities, not claims to them. You show E-E-A-T; you do not announce it.
