# Experience and expertise

Demonstrate experience by showing firsthand involvement: specific details, real examples, original photos or data, and the kind of knowledge that only comes from having done the thing. Demonstrate expertise through accuracy, depth, correct use of the subject's concepts, and a clear, identified author with relevant background.

## Showing experience

Firsthand experience shows in the details. Content from someone who has actually done the thing contains specifics, original examples, and observations that secondhand content lacks. Original photos, real data you gathered, and accounts of what actually happened all signal genuine experience. The reader and the systems can tell the difference between content written from having done something and content written from having read about it. Specificity born of experience is hard to fake.

## Showing expertise

Expertise shows in accuracy and depth. Content that uses the subject's concepts correctly, addresses the real complications rather than skating over them, and goes beyond the surface signals genuine knowledge. Errors, oversimplifications, and vagueness signal its absence. Demonstrating expertise means getting the substance right and going deep enough that someone knowledgeable would recognize the author as one of them. Depth and correctness are the evidence of expertise.

## Identify the author

A clear, identified author with relevant background strengthens both experience and expertise signals. Anonymous content gives the reader no one to evaluate; named content with a real, relevant bio lets the reader and the systems connect the content to a person with credentials and experience. Showing who wrote it, and why they are qualified, turns the content's quality into a signal attached to an identifiable, accountable source.

## Match the author to the topic

Experience and expertise are topic-specific: someone can be highly expert in one area and not another. The signal is strongest when the author's demonstrated experience and expertise match the topic at hand. Content on a subject written by someone with real, relevant background in that subject carries weight that the same person writing outside their area would not. Aligning who writes what with where their genuine experience and expertise lie is how the signal stays credible.
