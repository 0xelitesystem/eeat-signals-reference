# Authoritativeness

Authoritativeness is recognition from beyond your own site: other reputable sources citing you, linking to you, mentioning you, and treating you as a go-to source on your topic. Unlike experience and expertise, which you show in your content, authoritativeness is largely conferred by others.

## Recognition from others

Authoritativeness is what others say about you, not what you say about yourself. It shows when reputable sources in your field link to you, cite you, mention you, or recommend you as a source. This external recognition signals that people who know the subject regard you as a credible authority on it. Because it comes from others, it is harder to manufacture, which is exactly why it carries weight as a signal.

## Built through genuine standing

You build authoritativeness by genuinely becoming a source worth citing: doing good work, being known in your field, and earning mentions and links because your content deserves them. This is slow and cumulative, accruing as your body of work and reputation grow. There is no shortcut, because the signal is precisely the recognition that good standing earns over time. Authoritativeness is the visible result of having genuinely become an authority.

## Topic authority

Authoritativeness is strongest when concentrated on a clear topic. A site recognized as a go-to source on a specific subject has more authority on that subject than a site that covers everything shallowly. Building deep, connected content on a focused topic, and earning recognition for it, establishes topic authority that a scattered site cannot. Focus is what lets a small operator build real authority in a niche rather than diffuse weak signals across many.

## What does not build it

Authoritativeness is not built by self-declaration, by low-quality links acquired artificially, or by claiming status you have not earned. Systems and readers can distinguish genuine recognition from manufactured signals, and attempts to fake authority tend to backfire. The only durable path is the genuine one: become worth recognizing and let the recognition accumulate. Trying to shortcut authoritativeness usually wastes effort that genuine work would have rewarded.
